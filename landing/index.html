Every dictation tool solves the wrong half of this.

The typing speed was never the problem. SuperWhisper, Wispr Flow, and macOS Dictation are all fast at turning speech into text. None of them know who you're talking to. You still open WhatsApp, still find the chat, still leave what you were doing.

And the tools that do get you typing faster introduce a new failure: they quietly rewrite what you said. A real user, on Apple's own support forum, about a dictation tool changing his words after correctly hearing them:

“dictation gets my words correct but then it decides I must mean something else”

andersonkl, Apple Support Communities

One key, one sentence, one glance to confirm.

  1. 1

    Hold CtrlSpace

    A global hotkey registered with Carbon, no Accessibility permission needed just to listen.

  2. 2

    Say who and what

    “Message Pulkit that I will be late.” Your contacts load before you speak, so matching is biased toward names that matter to you.

  3. 3

    A card shows the matched contact and your exact words. Enter sends, Esc cancels, ⌘E edits first.

Two rules, both structural, not marketing.

Not settings you can turn off by accident. This is how the code is built.

It never rewrites your words.

The message body is sliced character-for-character out of what you actually said. Removing a stray “um” is allowed. Rewriting a sentence into something you didn't say is architecturally impossible, because there is no rewriting step in the code at all. Hinglish passes through untouched too, since byte offsets don't care what language you're speaking.

“some kind of stupid algorithm that is changing my words after they record correctly”

andersonkl, Apple Support Communities, on a competing dictation tool

It never guesses a recipient.

One clear match resolves and Voicy moves on. Two close matches, like two contacts named Rahul, and it asks instead of picking one. A wrong send on WhatsApp can't be undone, so the app stops and asks rather than guesses. Correct a match once and it remembers the mapping for next time.

Fully on-device

Transcription runs on Apple's on-device Speech framework. Contact matching runs against your local address book. Nothing is uploaded to make either of those work. Some competitors take the opposite approach:

“In their quest for ‘context awareness,’ Wispr Flow captures screenshots of your active window to ‘understand’ what you are doing.”

Ryan Shrott, Medium

Zero ban risk, because no WhatsApp API is touched

WhatsApp automation libraries reimplement its private protocol and get accounts permanently banned, sometimes after a handful of messages. Voicy never speaks to WhatsApp's servers. It hands macOS a whatsapp://send link, the same click-to-chat mechanism WhatsApp publishes for websites, which opens the real app with your message typed in. Nothing unusual to detect, because nothing unusual happened.

Incoming voice notes become readable text.

WhatsApp shipped on-device voice note transcription for phones in November 2024. Over a year later it still hasn't reached WhatsApp Desktop or Web. A four-minute voice note on your Mac still means listening to all four minutes. One person who hit this on desktop built his own fix instead:

“WhatsApp's voice note transcription on desktop is either missing entirely or very inaccurate for me. It skips words, struggles with accents, and sometimes just isn't available at all.”

jpxoi, Threads

Voicy's module for this reads voice note files already on your disk and turns them into text you can scan in seconds. Files never leave your machine, WhatsApp's servers are never touched. Plain statements, not a policy page.

Every line below is something you can check yourself by reading the source.

  1. Your voice never leaves your Mac. Transcription runs on-device with Apple's Speech framework. No server, no API key. Airplane mode changes nothing.
  2. No message content is ever stored or logged. The only thing saved is a name mapping, such as "Pulkit" pointing to a contact. Never the content of a message.
  3. Read-only on the WhatsApp folder. The voice-note module reads files that already exist on disk. It doesn't write to, move, or delete anything WhatsApp owns.
  4. Your contacts are never uploaded. They're read into memory to match a spoken name against, and that's the extent of it.
  5. The microphone opens when you hold the key and closes when you let go. No background listening, no screen recording, no keylogging.

Two permissions up front, nothing else.

Most tools in this category ask for Accessibility access before you've seen them do anything. Voicy asks for two things on first launch, and it's fully usable with just those two: Microphone, to hear you when you hold the hotkey, and Contacts, to turn a spoken name into a phone number.

Two optional upgrades, one toggle each, in Settings
Toggle What it unlocks
Hold a key to talk Push-to-talk on a bare modifier key. Needs Input Monitoring.
Send without pressing Enter Voicy presses Return for you after the confirm card appears. Needs Accessibility.

It works fully without either toggle: hotkey, transcription, contact matching, and the message pre-filled and ready in WhatsApp. You opt into each upgrade after you trust the app, not before you've tried it.

Questions worth answering plainly.

Will this get my WhatsApp banned?

No API is involved, so there's nothing for WhatsApp to detect as automation. Voicy hands macOS a whatsapp://send link, the same click-to-chat mechanism WhatsApp publishes for websites, which opens the real app with your message typed in. From WhatsApp's point of view, a real person opened the real app and pressed a real key.

Does my audio ever leave my Mac?

No. Transcription runs on Apple's on-device Speech framework. There's no server, no API key, and no upload step in the code.

Does it work offline?

Yes, for the hotkey, transcription, and contact matching, since all three run on-device. Sending still needs WhatsApp Desktop itself to deliver the message, the same as if you typed it yourself.

What about Indian English and Hinglish?

Indian names are genuinely hard for every speech engine. Measured on Voicy's own test harness: "Pulkit" comes back as "Polkit", "Polka", "Paul Kit"; "Aarav" as "our ab", "our ad". Voicy loads your contacts before you speak and biases matching toward the roughly 100 names that actually matter to you, then falls back to fuzzy and phonetic matching. Because the message body is sliced by character position rather than regenerated, Hinglish sentences pass through untouched too.

What macOS version do I need?

macOS 26 or later, on Apple Silicon, with WhatsApp Desktop installed. Only WhatsApp is wired up. Telegram and iMessage are not supported, and there's no Windows, Linux, or iOS build.

Is it free and open source?

Yes, MIT licensed on GitHub, built with zero third-party dependencies. There's no subscription and no download link, because there's no signed release yet: you clone the repository and build it yourself. That matches what people already ask for by name in the on-device dictation community:
