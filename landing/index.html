the whole idea

What happens to your sentence

Most dictation tools regenerate your text. A model reads what you said and writes something close to it. Voicy finds two spans in the transcript and cuts. The message body is the exact characters you spoke, taken by offset, never regenerated.

message Pulkit that I'll be late, leaving now
verb, dropped recipient, resolved against your contacts connector, dropped body, sliced by character offset and sent as-is

Because the body is a slice and not a rewrite, Hinglish survives. Byte offsets do not care what language you are speaking. The one edit Voicy will make is deleting a filler word, and even that is checked: the cleaned string is compared against the original, and if anything was added, changed or reordered instead of only removed, the untouched version is used.

The other half of the problem

Dictation is solved. SuperWhisper, Wispr Flow and macOS Dictation all turn speech into text quickly and well. None of them know who you are talking to, so you still CmdTab, still scroll past two hundred chats, still get pulled into three unread messages you did not come for. The typing was never the slow part. The context switch was.

The second half is trust. A tool that improves your sentence is a tool that occasionally puts words in your mouth, in a message with your name on it, that another person reads.

“dictation gets my words correct but then it decides I must mean something else”

Indian names break every speech engine

This is the hardest real problem in the app, and it is not fixed at the recognition step, because it cannot be. Here is what Apple's on-device recognizer actually returns for two names, measured on Voicy's own 22-clip audio suite:

Transcriptions observed across repeated runs of the same clips
SpokenWhat comes back
PulkitPolkit Polka Paul Kit Palka
AaravArav our ab our ad

The same clip can come back differently on consecutive runs. This is not a bug we can wait out.

Three documented ways to fix this at the source. All three did nothing.

  1. Contextual strings

    Feeding your contact names to the recognizer as hints, via AnalysisContext.contextualStrings. Ran the whole suite with hints on and off. Transcripts were byte-identical.

  2. The en_IN locale

    Already installed, no asset download needed. Byte-identical output on every clip, including every mangled name above, and about 1.5× slower.

  3. A custom language model

    SFCustomLanguageModelData compiled and attached, the documented mechanism for custom vocabulary. Byte-identical again, and it blew the latency budget at 1576 ms worst case.

So Voicy fixes names after recognition, where it actually has control. Fuzzy and phonetic matching recovers most of them, "Paul Kit" back to Pulkit. When it cannot, you correct the name once and Voicy stores that mapping, so "Polka" resolves to Pulkit instantly and permanently. Alias learning is not a nice extra here. It is the answer.

Two rules the code enforces

Not settings. There is no toggle for either of these, because neither is implemented as a check that could be skipped.

It cannot rewrite your words

There is no rewriting step in the pipeline. The body is a character range in the transcript, passed to the deep link unchanged. A language model is built and tested in this repo, and it is deliberately not wired in, because it adds punctuation you never spoke. Deterministic beats clever for the text of someone's message.

It will not guess a recipient

Two contacts named Rahul and it stops and asks. A name nobody has and it stops. A name the recognizer destroyed past recovery and it stops. A wrong send on WhatsApp cannot be recalled, so ambiguity resolves to a question, never to the most likely answer.

It never touches a WhatsApp API

Automation libraries that reimplement WhatsApp's private protocol get accounts permanently banned. Voicy hands macOS a whatsapp://send link, the click-to-chat scheme WhatsApp publishes for websites, and WhatsApp Desktop opens with the text already in the box. Nothing unusual to detect, because nothing unusual happened.

What leaves your Mac

Nothing.

  • Your voice. Transcription runs on Apple's on-device Speech framework. No server, no API key, no upload step in the code. Airplane mode changes nothing.
  • Your messages. Never stored, never logged, not even their length in an early version that got fixed. The only thing written to disk is a name mapping, "Polka" to a contact.
  • Your contacts. Read into memory to match a spoken name against. That is the whole extent of it.
  • The microphone. Opens when you hold the key, closes when you let go. No background listening, no screen capture, no keylogging.
  • The kill switch. A blocklist of numbers that can never be auto-sent to. If the file is present but corrupt, every auto-send is refused rather than allowed.

not shipped, and here is exactly why

Reading voice notes instead of listening to them

WhatsApp shipped on-device voice note transcription on phones in November 2024. More than a year later it still has not reached WhatsApp Desktop. A four minute voice note on your Mac still costs you four minutes.

Half of this is solved. macOS decodes WhatsApp's Ogg-Opus files natively, proven against real files from the container, read-only, no ffmpeg and no bundled decoder. The other half is not: six real voice notes we sampled were Hindi and Punjabi, and the English model turns those into fluent, confident nonsense.

Shipping this English-only would produce text that looks right and is wrong, about someone's actual message. That is worse than shipping nothing, so it is not wired into the app. It needs language selection first.

Questions

Will this get my WhatsApp banned?

No API is involved, so there is nothing to detect as automation. Voicy hands macOS a whatsapp://send link, the same mechanism behind every "chat on WhatsApp" button on the web. From WhatsApp's side, a person opened the app and pressed a key.

Does my audio leave my Mac?

No. On-device Speech framework, no server, no API key, no upload step in the code. You can check this yourself; it is the point of the repo being open.

Does it work offline?

The hotkey, transcription and contact matching do, all three run locally. Delivery still needs WhatsApp Desktop to be online, exactly as if you had typed the message yourself.

How fast is it, really?

Measured with pre-recorded speech injected into the real pipeline, not estimated: 31.5 ms from key-down to the microphone recording, and a median of 149 ms from end of speech to transcript across 22 clips. Method and caveats are on the pitch page.

What do I need to run it?

macOS 26 or later on Apple Silicon, with WhatsApp Desktop installed, and Xcode's Swift toolchain to build it. WhatsApp only. No Telegram, no iMessage, no Windows, no Linux, no iOS.

What is not finished?

Auto-send is written and tested but has never once been run end to end by a human, so treat it as untested. The ambiguous-contact picker, the edit-before-send shortcut and the Right Option hold-to-talk key have code and tests but no real-world use. Voice notes are decoded but not wired in. The pitch page lists all of it in one place.

It is a hotkey, a slice, and a confirm card.
